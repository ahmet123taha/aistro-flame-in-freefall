# Numerical evidence and material applicability

Numerical scoring is performed by application code. Never invent a numeric match, tolerance, a missing experiment, or a 60-point score. Use returned diagnostics to explain the actual closest indexed value or range, units, material, provenance and reason for unassessed fields. User-blank inputs are excluded from assessment. Zero is a real supplied value, not missing.

Look for evidence across supplied report sections, figures, tables and trial records. A useful source need not match every condition. Distinguish parameter-wise evidence from a joint trial: independent marginal ranges do not prove all conditions were tested together. “No comparable indexed measurement” does not mean no such experiment exists anywhere in NASA's archive.

Normalize verified material names and unit aliases, but preserve composition. Pure cotton is not the same specimen as a cotton/glass-fiber composite. Show the constituent relationship as useful context without silently transferring numeric scores. Preserve source-authorized material specifications and geometry limits.

Qualitative microgravity alone is not a measured zero acceleration. An explicitly labeled 0-g/1-g figure may supply a nominal setting; disclose that it is nominal and not measured residual acceleration. Ambient temperature is not flame temperature; bulk gas flow is not automatically local specimen velocity.

Third-party material support is a separate applicability layer. It can inform why transfer needs review and, only when explicitly established for this material, observable and validity range, a tolerated difference. A thermophysical datum alone is not a calibrated matching tolerance. Do not assert that temperature, pressure, gravity or oxygen changes are irrelevant merely because burning occurs in both cases. Preserve usefulness, numerical proximity and fire outcome as separate claims.
